<Tldr>
A migration copies a range from the donor to the recipient while the range stays writable, then holds writes to that range briefly while ownership switches on the config servers. With a read + update workload on the moving range, `moveRange` of **219,705 documents / 26.2 MB** took **1,555 ms**; median update latency went from **1.95 to 3.47 ms** during the move and the slowest update took **34 ms**, right at the commit. Afterwards the donor still holds the documents as **orphans**: deletion is delayed **900 s** (`orphanCleanupDelaySecs`). For those 15 minutes sh1 had **680,342 orphans** next to 319,658 owned documents. Queries through mongos stayed correct (a `SHARDING_FILTER` stage drops orphans), but they examined orphan keys, metadata counts reported 1,460,637 documents, and a query sent straight to a shard returned duplicates. The range deleter then removed 13 balancer ranges (460,637 documents) in about 30 s and the 219,705-document range in about 11–17 s.
</Tldr>

## Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Donor / recipient | The shard that owns the range now / the shard it moves to |
| Clone | Recipient copies the range's documents from the donor; writes continue on the donor and are forwarded |
| Critical section | Short final phase: writes to the range wait while the last changes are copied and the config servers record the new owner |
| Orphan | A document on a shard that does not own its range (left by a migration until cleanup) |
| SHARDING_FILTER | Plan stage on each shard that drops documents the shard does not own |
| Range deleter | Background task on the donor that deletes the old copy after `orphanCleanupDelaySecs` (default 900) |
| **✓ / ✗** | Allowed / held or filtered |

## The setup

`orders_s` (sharded on `seller_id`, balancing turned off for this collection) owned sellers 2303 and above on sh1. A workload picked 2,000 random orders of sellers 2303–3399 and, in a loop, read one by `seller_id` + `_id` and incremented a counter on it. After 8 s, `moveRange` moved sellers `[2303, 3400)` to sh2.

## Latency during the move

| Workload on sellers 2303–3399 | Reads median / p99 / max (ms) | Updates median / p99 / max (ms) |
|---|---|---|
| Before (2,580 each) | 0.37 / 1.63 / 8.1 | 1.95 / 7.03 / 22.8 |
| During the 1,555 ms move (323 each) | 0.67 / 3.42 / 5.7 | 3.47 / 10.91 / **33.8** |
| After (2,697 each) | 0.40 / 1.77 / 5.7 | 2.05 / 8.09 / 27.4 |

The balancer's own migrations of 4 MB ranges (Concept 2) took 199–447 ms each. A migration's cost grows with the range's size; the write pause at the end stays short because only the changes made during the copy are left to send.

## Orphans: who sees them

| Reader | Result for seller 100 (range moved to sh2), before cleanup |
|---|---|
| `countDocuments({ seller_id: 100 })` through mongos | 191 |
| `countDocuments({ seller_id: 100 })` directly on sh1 | 191 (all orphans) |
| `countDocuments({ seller_id: 100 })` directly on sh2 | 191 |
| `estimatedDocumentCount()` through mongos, whole collection | 1,460,637 (1,000,000 real) |

A backup, export or analytics job that connects to each shard directly and adds the results counts the 191 orders twice. Through mongos, each shard filters by the routing table it owns.

## The range deleter's own cost

During the 219,705-document deletion on sh1 (13:13:09.5 to about 13:13:26), a workload on sellers sh1 still owned (3400 and above) saw reads at median 0.71 ms against 0.41 ms before, and updates at 3.22 ms against 2.62 ms. The range deleter works in batches; the effect was visible but small at this size.

<Pitfall>
After a big rebalance, disk usage on the donor does not drop for 15 minutes, and then only inside WiredTiger's files: deleted documents free space for reuse, the files do not shrink. Do not judge a rebalance by disk graphs in the first quarter hour, and never read shards directly (mongodump per shard, `--host` straight to a shard member) for anything that must be exact while orphans exist. `numOrphanDocs` in `$collStats` and `config.rangeDeletions` on each shard show what is pending.
</Pitfall>

## Real-world scenario: the nightly per-shard export

An analytics pipeline exports each shard's `orders` directly, to spread load, and sums the files. The night after a rebalance, GMV is 46% higher than the dashboards. The rebalance moved ranges at 23:55; the exports ran at 00:00, inside the 900 s window, and counted every moved order on both the old and new shard.

| Fix | Effect |
|---|---|
| Export through mongos (or `$out`/`$merge` from mongos) | Each shard filters orphans |
| If per-shard reads are needed, filter by the ranges the shard owns in `config.chunks` | Duplicates excluded |
| Check `config.rangeDeletions` is empty on every shard before exporting | Detects pending orphans |
| Disable balancing during the export window for that collection | No new orphans appear mid-export |

## Key takeaways

1. A migration copies the range while it stays readable and writable, then holds writes to that range briefly to switch ownership.
2. 219,705 documents moved in 1.56 s; median update latency rose from 1.95 to 3.47 ms during the move; the worst write took 34 ms, at the commit.
3. The donor keeps the moved documents as orphans for `orphanCleanupDelaySecs` (900 s by default).
4. Through mongos, `SHARDING_FILTER` hides orphans, but shards still examine them; direct shard reads and metadata counts include them.
5. The range deleter removed 460,637 orphans in about 30 s and 219,705 in about 11–17 s, with a small latency effect on the donor.
