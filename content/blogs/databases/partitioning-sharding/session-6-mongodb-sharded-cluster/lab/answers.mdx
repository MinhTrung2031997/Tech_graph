<Tldr>
A sharded cluster routes by metadata, moves data in ranges, cleans up later, and rewrites whole collections to change keys. Each step has a measurable cost.
</Tldr>

## Q1. Sharded but not spread

**Question:** you sharded a 300 MB collection on a good key and it is still 100% on one shard. Why, and what is the per-collection fix?

Sharding a loaded collection creates one chunk on the primary shard. The balancer moves data only when shards differ by three chunk sizes, 384 MB with the 128 MB default, so a 300 MB collection is "balanced" where it is. In the lab the 113 MiB `orders_s` stayed 100% on sh1 with `balancerCompliant: true`. Fix for that collection alone: `configureCollectionBalancing` with a smaller `chunkSize` (4 MB moved 13 ranges in 16 s).

> **Common mistake:** changing the cluster-wide chunk size in `config.settings`, which affects every collection.

## Q2. What mongos needs

**Question:** what does mongos need to route `find({ seller_id: 4321 })` to one shard, and where does that information live?

The collection's shard key (`config.collections`) and the list of ranges with their owners (`config.chunks`), both on the config servers. mongos caches them as the routing table, finds the range containing 4321 (`[2303, MaxKey)`, sh1), and sends the query there only: `SINGLE_SHARD`, 212 documents. If a migration has changed the owner since, the shard rejects the stale version and mongos refreshes.

> **Common mistake:** expecting the config servers to be on the path of every query.

## Q3. A fast broadcast

**Question:** a lookup by `_id` on a collection sharded by `seller_id` runs in 0.3 ms on a 2-shard cluster. Why is it still a scaling problem?

Because it runs on every shard: the profiler counted 500 queries on sh1 **and** 500 on sh2 for 500 lookups, against 272 + 228 when `seller_id` was included. On two shards next to each other this costs microseconds. With N shards it is N queries per lookup; the slowest shard sets the latency, and adding shards adds no capacity for these queries.

> **Common mistake:** judging a shard key on a two-shard test cluster by latency alone.

## Q4. When writes are held during a migration

**Question:** during a migration, when are writes to the moving range held, and for how long was that in the lab?

Only in the critical section at the end, while the last changes are copied and the config servers record the new owner. Moving 219,705 documents took 1,555 ms; updates to the range kept running throughout (median 3.47 ms vs 1.95 ms before), and the slowest, 34 ms, came at +1,517 ms, at the switch.

> **Common mistake:** pausing the application for migrations. They are designed to run under load.

## Q5. Orphans

**Question:** give two ways orphan documents can mislead you, and how long they stay by default.

Any two of:
- Metadata counts include them: `estimatedDocumentCount()` and `count()` returned 1,460,637 for 1,000,000 orders.
- Direct reads of a shard include them: seller 100's 191 orders appeared on both sh1 and sh2.
- Queries through mongos are correct but slower: sh1 examined 41,040 keys to return 22,209.
- Disk on the donor does not drop after a rebalance until the range deleter runs.

They stay `orphanCleanupDelaySecs`, 900 s by default, plus the deletion itself (about 30 s for 460,637 documents, 11–17 s for 219,705 in the lab).

> **Common mistake:** "fixing" duplicates in a per-shard export by de-duplicating on `_id` instead of reading through mongos.

## Q6. Five minutes vs five seconds

**Question:** why did `moveCollection` of 1M documents take more than five minutes, while `movePrimary` took five seconds?

`moveCollection` is built on resharding, which never finishes its cloning phase before `reshardingMinimumOperationDurationMillis` (300,000 ms): 301 s cloning, 30 s applying, 0.96 s blocking writes, 0.15 s committing, 332.7 s in all. It keeps the collection writable until the last second. `movePrimary` simply copies the untracked collections and switches, in 5.0–5.3 s, but rejects writes meanwhile.

> **Common mistake:** concluding that `movePrimary` is the better tool because it is faster.

## Q7. movePrimary and writes

**Question:** what does `movePrimary` do to writes, and what must the application do about it?

Writes to the database's untracked unsharded collections fail with code 319, "movePrimary is in progress for namespace …", for the whole copy: 8,626 failed updates in 4.9 s in the lab. Reads continue. The application must catch the error and retry with backoff (or the operation must run in a window where those collections take no writes); a loop without error handling died on the first failure.

> **Common mistake:** assuming writes queue as they do in a migration's critical section.

## Q8. Refine or reshard

**Question:** when is `refineCollectionShardKey` enough, and when do you need `reshardCollection`?

Refine when the current key's leading field is right but too coarse: appending a field (`seller_id` → `seller_id, created_at`) adds split points, keeps every targeted query targeted, and is metadata only (110 ms after a 1.2 s index build). Reshard when the leading field is wrong: queries do not filter on it, it is monotonic, or it has a few huge values you cannot route around. Resharding rewrites the collection: 332 s for 1M orders, with a second copy's worth of disk and a 0.53 s write block at the end.

> **Common mistake:** reaching for `reshardCollection` first. Check whether a refine solves the actual problem.
