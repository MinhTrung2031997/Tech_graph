<ArticleStack>
<SplitPair>
<SplitLead>

## Development simplicity

</SplitLead>
<SplitCol title="Monolithic">

Easier to develop and debug as everything is in a single codebase.

</SplitCol>
<SplitCol title="Microservices">

More complex due to multiple services and inter-service communication.

</SplitCol>
</SplitPair>
<SplitPair>
<SplitLead>

## Deployment

</SplitLead>
<SplitCol title="Monolithic">

A single deployment unit makes it easier to deploy but requires redeploying the entire application for any change.

</SplitCol>
<SplitCol title="Microservices">

Independent deployments allow faster releases, but deployment complexity increases.

</SplitCol>
</SplitPair>
<SplitPair>
<SplitLead>

## Scalability

</SplitLead>
<SplitCol title="Monolithic">

Horizontal scaling is possible, but it scales the entire application rather than specific components.

</SplitCol>
<SplitCol title="Microservices">

Independent scaling of services based on demand, optimizing resource usage.

</SplitCol>
</SplitPair>
<SplitPair>
<SplitLead>

## Technology flexibility

</SplitLead>
<SplitCol title="Monolithic">

Limited to a single technology stack, making development uniform but less flexible.

</SplitCol>
<SplitCol title="Microservices">

Different services can use different technologies, allowing for optimized performance and innovation.

</SplitCol>
</SplitPair>
<SplitPair>
<SplitLead>

## Fault isolation

</SplitLead>
<SplitCol title="Monolithic">

A failure in one module can affect the entire application, making it less resilient.

</SplitCol>
<SplitCol title="Microservices">

Failures are isolated to individual services, reducing the impact on the whole system.

</SplitCol>
</SplitPair>
<SplitPair>
<SplitLead>

## Team autonomy

</SplitLead>
<SplitCol title="Monolithic">

A single codebase makes collaboration easier but can cause bottlenecks as teams grow.

</SplitCol>
<SplitCol title="Microservices">

Teams can work independently on different services, improving efficiency and agility.

</SplitCol>
</SplitPair>
<SplitPair>
<SplitLead>

## Performance optimization

</SplitLead>
<SplitCol title="Monolithic">

Performance tuning applies to the whole application rather than specific components.

</SplitCol>
<SplitCol title="Microservices">

Each service can be optimized individually, improving system-wide efficiency.

</SplitCol>
</SplitPair>
<SplitPair>
<SplitLead>

## Security

</SplitLead>
<SplitCol title="Monolithic">

Easier to manage security since everything is within a single application boundary.

</SplitCol>
<SplitCol title="Microservices">

More challenging due to distributed services, requiring extra effort for authentication and data security.

</SplitCol>
</SplitPair>
<SplitPair>
<SplitLead>

## Maintenance & updates

</SplitLead>
<SplitCol title="Monolithic">

Simpler maintenance since all components are in one place but requires full redeployment for changes.

</SplitCol>
<SplitCol title="Microservices">

Individual services can be updated without affecting others, reducing downtime and improving maintainability.

</SplitCol>
</SplitPair>
<SplitPair>
<SplitLead>

## Testing & debugging

</SplitLead>
<SplitCol title="Monolithic">

Easier debugging and testing within a single environment.

</SplitCol>
<SplitCol title="Microservices">

More challenging due to distributed services, requiring observability tools.

</SplitCol>
</SplitPair>
<SplitPair>
<SplitLead>

## Business agility

</SplitLead>
<SplitCol title="Monolithic">

Slower innovation due to the need to modify and redeploy the entire application.

</SplitCol>
<SplitCol title="Microservices">

Faster time-to-market with independent feature releases and updates.

</SplitCol>
</SplitPair>
<SplitPair>
<SplitLead>

## Data management

</SplitLead>
<SplitCol title="Monolithic">

A single database simplifies transactions and consistency.

</SplitCol>
<SplitCol title="Microservices">

Each service may have its own database, leading to complexity in data consistency and transactions.

</SplitCol>
</SplitPair>
<SplitPair>
<SplitLead>

## When to choose

</SplitLead>
<SplitCol title="Choose monolithic if">

- You have a small team and want quick development.
- The application is relatively simple and does not require frequent scaling.
- You want simpler deployment and maintenance.

</SplitCol>
<SplitCol title="Choose microservices if">

- You expect high scalability and independent service evolution.
- Different teams manage different functionalities and require flexibility.
- You need fault isolation and faster innovation cycles.

</SplitCol>
