## Biggest challenge

What was the biggest challenge you faced, and how did you overcome it?

One of the biggest challenges I faced was when I first joined CyberLogitec and started working on a vessel logistics system.

The business domain was extremely complex—much more complicated than anything I had worked on before. It involved many workflows, rules, and dependencies, and I quickly realized that understanding the domain required more than just technical skills.

At the same time, there was quite a lot of pressure, because I needed to deliver tasks on time while still learning the system. I also understood that without a solid grasp of the business logic, it would be very difficult to implement correct and reliable solutions.

To overcome this, I took a structured and disciplined approach. First, I focused heavily on understanding the business domain. I proactively reached out to teammates, partners, and domain experts to learn how the system worked end-to-end. I asked a lot of questions, joined discussions, and continuously clarified my assumptions.

To reinforce my understanding, I also documented key processes and created diagrams to visualize workflows and dependencies. This helped me build a clearer mental model of the system over time.

At the same time, I leveraged my technical strengths to adapt quickly to the existing technologies, which allowed me to reduce the time spent on technical ramp-up and focus more on understanding the business side.

I also managed complexity by breaking tasks down into smaller, manageable pieces and prioritizing them based on business impact and deadlines. This helped me stay focused and make consistent progress without feeling overwhelmed.

In addition, I maintained regular communication with my team and stakeholders. I made sure to ask for clarification whenever something was unclear, and I also invested extra time outside of work to study the domain and related systems.

As a result, I was able to ramp up quickly, deliver tasks on time, and gain confidence working with complex business logic. This experience significantly improved my ability to handle complex systems in a structured and effective way.

## Urgent deadlines

How do you prioritize when you have multiple urgent deadlines?

When I have multiple urgent deadlines, I start by evaluating the priority of each task based on business impact, urgency, and dependencies.

Since I work closely with Business Analysts, I usually align with them first to clarify priorities and ensure I fully understand the business expectations behind each task. This helps me focus on what brings the most value to the product.

I also identify which tasks are critical or blocking others, so I can handle them first and avoid delays in the overall workflow.

If priorities /praɪˈɔːr.ə.t̬i/ are unclear or conflicting /kənˈflɪk.tɪŋ/, I proactively discuss with the BA or stakeholders to align on what should be delivered first.

In addition, I break down tasks into smaller pieces and plan my work step by step, so I can stay focused and make steady progress even under pressure.

I also keep communication transparent, especially if there are risks or potential delays, so the team can adjust early.

I believe effective prioritization comes from strong alignment with business stakeholders and focusing on delivering the highest impact, rather than just trying to do everything at once.

## Incomplete information

Have you ever had to make a decision /dɪˈsɪʒ.ən/ without enough information?

Yes, there have been situations where I had to make decisions without having complete information.

In those cases, I first try to gather as much relevant context as possible within a limited time, including checking existing documentation, code, and discussing briefly with teammates or the BA if needed.

Then, I evaluate the possible options and make a decision based on the best available information, focusing on minimizing risks and aligning with business priorities.

When making that decision, I usually choose an approach that is safe and flexible, so it can be adjusted later if new information becomes available.

I also make sure to communicate my assumptions clearly to the team, so everyone is aware and can provide feedback if needed.

**I believe making decisions under uncertainty is part of the job, and the key is to balance speed, risk, and adaptability.**

## Technical decisions

How do you make technical decisions?

When making technical decisions, I usually follow a structured approach.

First, I make sure I clearly understand the requirements and the business context, often by aligning with the Business Analyst or stakeholders. This helps ensure that the solution actually solves the right problem.

Then, I evaluate different options by considering key factors such as scalability, maintainability, performance, and development effort. I also think about trade-offs, especially between short-term delivery and long-term sustainability.

If needed, I discuss these options with the team to gather different perspectives and validate the approach.

Once a decision is made, I try to choose a solution that is simple, practical, and flexible enough to adapt if requirements change.

I believe good technical decisions are not about choosing the most complex solution, but about selecting the one that best balances business needs, technical quality, and long-term impact.

## A project that failed

Describe a time a project failed or didn't go as planned. What did you do?

There was a situation where a feature didn’t go as planned due to unclear requirements at the beginning.

We initially implemented the solution based on our understanding, but later, after feedback from the Business Analyst and stakeholders, we realized that some key business rules were misunderstood. This led to rework and impacted our delivery timeline.

When that happened, I focused on addressing the issue rather than assigning blame. I worked closely with the BA to clarify the requirements in detail and ensured that all assumptions were clearly documented and aligned.

I also reviewed the implementation with the team to identify what needed to be adjusted and prioritized the rework to minimize further delays.

More importantly, I learned from this experience. After that, I became more proactive in clarifying requirements early, asking more detailed questions, and validating understanding before starting implementation.

**I believe that setbacks like this are valuable, because they help improve both the development process and how we collaborate with stakeholders in the long term.**

## Unclear requirements

What do you do when requirements are unclear?

When requirements are unclear, I don’t start implementing right away. Instead, I focus on clarifying them as early as possible.

Since I work closely with Business Analysts /ˈæn.ə.lɪst/, I usually start by discussing with them to better understand the business context, expected outcomes, and any missing details.

I also ask specific questions and try to break down the requirements into smaller parts to identify gaps or inconsistencies.

If needed, I document assumptions or propose a possible solution and validate it with the BA or stakeholders before moving forward.

In some cases, I suggest implementing a small initial version of the feature that focuses on the core logic or the most critical flow.

The goal is to validate key assumptions early with the BA or stakeholders, instead of waiting until the full implementation is complete.

This early feedback helps us quickly identify any misunderstandings or gaps in the requirements, so we can adjust before investing too much effort.

**I believe that investing time to clarify requirements early helps avoid rework and ensures we deliver the right solution.**

## Bug or not

How do you determine whether an issue is actually a bug or not?

When I encounter an issue, the first thing I do is try to clearly understand the expected behavior based on the requirements, acceptance criteria, or business logic.

Then, I reproduce the issue in a controlled environment to confirm whether it is consistent and not caused by temporary factors.

After that, I compare the actual behavior with the expected behavior. If there is a mismatch, I start investigating further by checking logs, data flow, and related components to identify the root cause.

I also verify whether the behavior might be intended or caused by a misunderstanding of the requirements, so I often align with the Business Analyst or stakeholders if anything is unclear.

If needed, I check edge cases and recent changes to see if they could have introduced the issue.

I believe the key is to validate both the technical behavior and the business expectation before concluding that something is a bug.
