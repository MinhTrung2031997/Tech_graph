## Beyond the job

Tell me about a time you went beyond your job description to help the team

One example was when our team was approaching a tight deadline, and the QA team was overloaded with testing tasks.

Although testing was not part of my main responsibility, I stepped in to support by helping review test cases, verifying critical flows, and reproducing issues reported by QA.

At the same time, I also worked closely with them to quickly fix bugs and ensure the fixes were properly validated before release.

In addition, I helped clarify some business logic with the Business Analyst to make sure both developers and QA had the same understanding.

As a result, we were able to stabilize the release and deliver on time without compromising quality.

**I believe going beyond my role when needed is important to support the team and ensure the overall success of the project.**

## Process improvement

Have you ever proposed and implemented a process improvement? What was the impact?

Yes, I’ve proposed and implemented a process improvement related to how we handle requirements and development flow.

Previously, we sometimes faced rework because requirements were not fully clarified before development started. This caused misunderstandings between developers, QA, and the Business Analyst /ˈæn.ə.lɪst/.

To improve this, I suggested introducing a more structured pre-alignment step before implementation. In this step, developers review user stories early, raise questions, and align closely with the BA on business logic and acceptance criteria.

I also encouraged documenting key assumptions and using simple diagrams to clarify complex flows, so everyone had a shared understanding before coding.

After applying this approach, we saw fewer misunderstandings, reduced rework, and more predictable delivery. It also improved collaboration between developers, QA, and BA.

**I believe small process improvements like this can have a significant impact on both team efficiency and product quality over time.**

## Start to finish

Describe a time you took ownership of a project from start to finish.

One example was when I took full ownership of a feature in our IoT alert system, from requirement clarification to production release.

At the beginning, I worked closely with the Business Analyst to fully understand the requirements, clarify business rules, and define the expected outcomes. I also identified some gaps and edge cases early and aligned them before starting development.

Then, I designed the solution, focusing on making it scalable and maintainable, since the system processes data from sensors continuously. After that, I implemented the feature and ensured proper handling of different conditions and thresholds.

During development, I actively communicated with the team, participated in code reviews, and made sure the implementation aligned with our standards.

I also coordinated with the QA team to ensure proper test coverage, supported testing, and quickly resolved any issues that came up.

After deployment, I continued to monitor the system to ensure it worked correctly in production and handled any issues if needed.

As a result, the feature was delivered successfully and helped clients monitor their shipments more effectively, reducing the risk of damage during transit.

**I believe taking ownership from start to finish means being responsible not just for delivery, but for the overall success and reliability of the solution.**
