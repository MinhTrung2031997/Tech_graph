## Keep skills current

How do you keep your technical skills up to date?

I keep my technical skills up to date through a combination /ˌkɑːm.bəˈneɪ.ʃən/ of continuous learning and practical application.

I regularly follow technical blogs, documentation, and community discussions to stay aware of new trends and best practices.

More importantly, I try to apply what I learn in real projects whenever possible. For example, when I learned about event-driven architecture, I applied it using Kafka to improve system scalability in my recent work.

I also learn a lot through code reviews and discussions with teammates, as they often bring different perspectives and experiences.

In addition, when I have time, I explore new technologies or build small prototypes to deepen my understanding.

Overall, I focus on learning things that are practical and can bring real value to the system I’m working on.

## Technical mistake

What's a technical mistake you made, and what did you learn from it?

One technical mistake I made earlier in my career was over-engineering a solution.

I designed a feature with a more complex architecture than necessary, thinking it would make the system more scalable and flexible in the future. However, in reality, the requirements were not that complex, and the solution made the code harder to understand and maintain.

As a result, it slowed down development and made it more difficult for other team members to work with the code.

When I realized this, I worked with the team to simplify /ˈsɪm.plə.faɪ/ the design and refactor the implementation to better match the actual requirements.

From that experience, I learned the importance of choosing the right level of complexity and focusing on simplicity /sɪmˈplɪs.ə.t̬i/ and clarity first.

I believe good engineering is not about building the most advanced solution, but about building the most appropriate one for the problem.

## Good software engineer

How to become a good software engineer.

### Set Clear Goals

Think about the kind of software engineer you want to become. What skills or qualities do you admire in others? Set goals that align with that vision.

### Build Good Habits

Success comes from discipline. Create a routine that helps you learn, grow, and stay consistent.

### Keep Learning

Technology changes fast. Make it a habit to explore new tools and frameworks whenever you can. Even a few minutes of practice during your free time adds up.

### Learn from Mistakes

Don’t be afraid to make mistakes—they’re part of the learning process. Reflect on them, adjust, and move forward.

### Adapt Quickly

Be open to new ideas and ready to dive into new technologies. Flexibility is key in this field.

### Communicate and Collaborate

Talk to other developers, share ideas, and ask questions. You’ll learn a lot by working with and learning from others.

## Next 3–5 years

Where do you see yourself in the next 3–5 years?

In the next 3 to 5 years, I see myself growing into a stronger senior engineer, with deeper expertise in system design and building scalable, maintainable systems.

I want to take on more responsibility in designing solutions, making technical decisions /dɪˈsɪʒ.ən/, and contributing to the overall architecture of the system.

At the same time, I would like to expand my impact by mentoring junior developers and helping improve team practices and code quality.

Over time, I’m also interested in moving toward a technical leadership role, where I can contribute not only at the code level but also in shaping technical direction.

I believe the most important goal is to continuously grow while delivering real value to the team and the product.
