MAN: Good morning, is this seat taken?

WOMAN: No, please sit down. I was just enjoying my coffee and reading

MAN: Thank you so much. This coffee shop is always so crowded in the mornings. My name is Alex, by the way

WOMAN: Nice to meet you, Alex. I’m Sarah. Yeah, this place is popular because they make the best cappuccinos  /ˌkæp.əˈtʃiː.noʊ/ in the neighborhood

MAN: I completely agree. I’ve been coming here for six months now. Do you live nearby?

WOMAN: Yes, I moved to this area last year. I live in the apartment building just around the corner, the one with the red brick facade  /fəˈsɑːd/

MAN: Oh, I know that building, it looks beautiful from the outside. I live about 10 minutes away on foot. How do you like living here?

WOMAN: I really love it, the neighborhood is so peaceful, and there are plenty of parks and shops. Where are you originally /əˈrɪdʒ.ən.əl.i/ from? I can hear a slight accent

MAN: You have a good ear. I’m originally from Toronto  /təˈrɑːn.t̬oʊ/, Canada  /ˈkæn.ə.də/, and I moved here 3 years ago for work. What about you?

WOMAN: I’m from a small town in Colorado  /ˌkɑː.ləˈrɑː.doʊ/. I came here to pursue my career in graphic design. The opportunities here are much better than in my hometown

MAN: That’s exciting; graphic design must be very creative. What kind of project do you work on?

WOMAN: I work for a marketing agency. So I design everything from website layouts to social media campaigns and company logos  /ˈloʊ.ɡoʊ/. Every project is different, which keeps things interesting

MAN: That must require a lot of imagination  /ɪˌmædʒ.əˈneɪ.ʃən/. I work in software development, so I spend most of my time writing code and solving technical problems

WOMAN: Software development sounds challenging. Do you enjoy it?

MAN: Most days, yes, I like the problem-solving aspect of it. When you finally figure out why something isn’t working, it’s very satisfying, but sometimes it can be frustrating when you’re stuck on the bug for hours

WOMAN: I can imagine, I have similar experiences with design, sometimes I stare at a blank  /blæŋk/ screen for an hour, trying to come up with the perfect concept

MAN: Exactly, creative work can be difficult. Do you have any hobbies outside of work

WOMAN: I love photography /fəˈtɑː.ɡrə.fi/, on weekends I usually take my camera  /ˈkæm.rə/ and explore different parts of the city, I especially enjoy taking photos of architecture and street scenes

MAN: That’s wonderful. Photography complements your design work perfectly. I’m actually looking for a new hobby myself. I spend too much time indoors

WOMAN: You should try photography, or maybe hiking, there are some beautiful trails about an hour from here

MAN: Hiking sounds interesting. I used to hike a lot when I lived in Canada. Do you go hiking often?

WOMAN: I try to go at least once a month. There is a hiking group that meets every Saturday morning. Would you like me to send you the information?

MAN: That would be great, I’d love to join sometime. Do you need special equipment?

WOMAN: Not really, just comfortable shoes, water, and weather-appropriate clothing. The trails we usually take are suitable for beginners

MAN: Perfect, I'll definitely check it out. By the way, have you had breakfast yet? I’m thinking about ordering something

WOMAN: I only had coffee so far, their blueberry muffins are delicious if you’re looking for recommendations

MAN: I’ll order one. Would you like anything?

WOMAN: A croissant would be nice, thank you.

MAN: After ordering. So, Sarah, have you traveled much since moving here?

WOMAN:  Not as much as I’d like. Last summer, I visited San Francisco for a week, and the city was amazing, especially the Golden Gate Bridge and Fisherman’s Wharf

MAN: San Francisco is beautiful. I went there two years ago for the technology conference. Did you take many photographs

WOMAN: Hundreds, I filled up my entire camera’s memory card. The architecture there is so diverse and colorful

MAN: I can imagine. I want to travel more, but work keeps me quite busy. Do you speak any other languages besides English?

WOMAN: I studied Spanish in school for 4 years, but I’m not very fluent. I can understand the basic conversations and read simple texts

MAN: That’s more than me, I only speak English and a little French from high school, I have forgotten most of it though.

WOMAN: Languages are difficult to maintain if you don’t practice regularly. Do you have family back in Canada

MAN:  Yes, my parents and younger sister still live in Toronto. I try to visit them twice a year, usually during Christmas and summer

WOMAN: That must be hard being so far away. Do you video call them often?

MAN:  Every Sunday evening. Technology makes it easier, but I still miss being able to see them in person regularly

WOMAN: I understand completely. My family is also far away, and I miss them a lot, but I call my mom almost every day

MAN: That’s sweet. Family connections are important. Do you have any siblings?

WOMAN: I have one older brother, he’s a teacher in Denver. We’re very close despite the distance

MAN: Teaching is such an important profession. What subject does he teach?

WOMAN: He teaches high school mathematics. He’s very passionate about helping students understand the complex concept

MAN: Mathematics was always challenging for me in school. I preferred subjects like science and computer programming

WOMAN: Everyone has different strengths. I was better at art and English literature  /ˈlɪt̬.ɚ.ə.tʃɚ/. Math was definitely my favorite subject

MAN: Haha, at least you’re honest about it. What kind of books do you enjoy reading?

WOMAN: I love mystery novels and historical fiction. Right now, I’m reading a book about ancient Rome

MAN: Historical fiction is fascinating. I prefer science fiction and fantasy  /ˈfæn.tə.si/ novels. They let you escape to completely different worlds

WOMAN: I enjoy those too. Have you read any good books recently?

MAN: Yes, I just finished a science fiction series  /ˈsɪr.iːz/ about space exploration /ˌek.spləˈreɪ.ʃən/. It was incredibly imaginative and thought-provoking

WOMAN: That sounds interesting. Maybe you can recommend it to me sometime

MAN: That’s interesting. What do you usually do on the weekends besides hiking and photography

WOMAN: I enjoy cooking new recipes, and I try to learn dishes from different cultures. Last weekend, I made Thai green curry for the first time

MAN: How did it turn out?

WOMAN: Better than expected; it was spicy but delicious. Do you cook much?

MAN: I try to, but I’m not very skilled. I can make basic meals like pasta and stir-fry. Anything more complicated usually ends in disaster

WOMAN: Cooking just takes practice. Maybe you can start with the simple recipes and gradually try more complex dishes

MAN:  That’s good advice. Do you have a favorite cuisine?

WOMAN: Italian food is probably my favorite. I love pizza /ˈpiːt.sə/, pasta  /ˈpɑː.stə/, and risotto

/rɪˈzɑː.t̬oʊ/. What about you?

MAN: I really enjoy Japanese  /ˌdʒæp.ənˈiːz/ food, especially sushi and ramen. There’s a great Japanese restaurant near my apartment

WOMAN: I love sushi too. Maybe we could go together sometime

MAN: That would be wonderful. Are you free next weekend?

WOMAN: Let me check my schedule. Yes, Saturday evening works for me

MAN: Perfect, I’ll make a reservation. Do you have any dietary  ˈdaɪ.ə.ter.i/ restrictions I should know about

WOMAN: No, I eat everything. How about you?

MAN: Same here. I’m quite adventurous  /ədˈven.tʃɚ.əs/ with food

WOMAN: Great. I’m looking forward to it. So Alex, I’m curious what made you decide to move here from Toronto. It’s quite a big change

MAN: It was primarily for my career. I received a job offer from a tech company here that I couldn’t refuse. The salary was better, and the project was more interesting than what I was doing in Toronto

WOMAN: That makes sense. Was it difficult leaving everything behind and starting fresh in a new city

MAN: Very difficult at first. The first few months were lonely. I didn’t know anyone. I spent most evenings alone in my apartment, but gradually, I adjusted

WOMAN: I went through something similar when I moved here. The hardest part was not having my close friend anymore

MAN: Exactly, you can just call someone to meet for coffee or dinner like you used to. Everything requires planning now.

WOMAN: True, but technology helps. I still have weekly video calls with my best friend from Colorado

MAN: Same here. My best friend from Toronto and I play online games every Friday night; it’s our way of staying connected

WOMAN: That’s a great idea. What games do you play?

MAN: Mostly strategy games and some multiplayer adventures. It’s fun because you can chat while playing, so it feels like we’re hanging out

WOMAN: I should try that with my friends. I’m not much of a gamer, though. The only game I play occasionally is chess on my phone

MAN: Chess is excellent for your brain. I used to play when I was younger, but I haven’t played in years

WOMAN: We could play sometime if you want to get back into it. There’s a nice park nearby with outdoor chess tables

MAN: I’d like that, though I warn you I’m probably very rusty  /ˈrʌs.ti/

WOMAN: Don’t worry. I’m not a master either; it’s just for fun

MAN: Speaking of the park, is that the one with the big fountain in the center?

WOMAN: Yeah, exactly, Riverside Park. It’s beautiful, especially during the spring when all the flowers bloom

MAN: I have walked through it a few times. They have concerts there during the summer, don’t they?

WOMAN: They do. Every Saturday evening from June through August. They bring in different bands and musicians /mjuːˈzɪʃ.ən/. Have you been to any?

MAN: Not yet, but I’d love to. What kind of music do they usually have?

WOMAN: It varies: jazz, classical, rock, and folk music. Last summer, I saw the fantastic jazz band there

MAN: Jazz is wonderful for outdoor concerts. The relaxed atmosphere suits the music perfectly

WOMAN: Absolutely, people bring blankets and picnic baskets; it’s very communal and friendly

MAN: That sounds like a perfect summer evening. We should definitely go to this one this year

WOMAN: Let’s plan on it. I will let you know when the schedule is published. They usually announce it in May

MAN: Excellent. So, Sarah, what do you want to do during winter? The hiking must be more challenging then

WOMAN: Winter is trickier. Yes, I do some indoor activities like going to museums and art galleries. There’s an excellent contemporary  /kənˈtem.pə.rer.i/ art museum downtown

MAN: I have heard about it but haven’t visited yet. Is it worth going?

WOMAN: Definitely, they have rotating exhibitions. There’s always something new. Last month, they had a photography exhibition that was incredible

MAN:  I should visit soon. I don’t go to the museum as often as I should

WOMAN: Most people don’t. We get busy with daily life and forget about cultural activities

MAN: That’s very true. I keep telling myself I will be more cultural, but then weekends arrive, and I just want to relax at home

WOMAN: I understand that feeling completely. Sometimes doing nothing is exactly what you need after a busy week

MAN: Exactly. Do you watch much television or movies

WOMAN: I watch some shows, mostly on streaming services. I’m currently watching a British crime series, and it’s very addictive

MAN: British crime shows are excellent. I love the characters and the complex plots

WOMAN: Me too. The writing is usually superior /səˈpɪr.i.ɚ/ to American shows. What kind of show do you watch?

MAN: I enjoy documentaries /ˌdɑː.kjəˈmen.t̬ɚ.i/, especially about science and nature. I find them educational  /ˌedʒ.əˈkeɪ.ʃən.əl/ and relaxing at the same time

WOMAN: Nature documentaries are beautiful. The cinematography  /ˌsɪn.ə.məˈtɑː.ɡrə.fi/ is always stunning

MAN: Have you seen the one about oceans? The underwater footage is absolutely breathtaking

WOMAN: I think I started watching it, but never finished. I should go back to it

MAN: You definitely should. It shows creatures from the deepest parts of the ocean that look almost alien /ˈeɪ.li.ən/

WOMAN: That sounds fascinating. I will add it to my watch list

MAN: So, tell me about your typical work day. What time do you usually start?

WOMAN: I try to get to the office by 9:00. I usually spend the first hour checking emails and planning my day

MAN: That’s a good routine. Do you work on one project at a time or multiple projects simultaneously

WOMAN: Usually multiple projects. Right now, I work on three different campaigns for three different clients

MAN: That requires excellent organization skills

WOMAN: It does. I use a lot of lists and calendar reminders, otherwise I would forget important deadlines

MAN: I’m the same way; without my task management app, I would be completely lost

WOMAN: Which app do you use? I’m always looking for better tools

MAN: I use one called Taskmaster; it’s simple but effective. You can organize tasks by project and set priorities

WOMAN: Taskmaster is much better for complex projects. They have a free version you can try

MAN: Thanks for the recommendation. So, what time do you usually finish work?

WOMAN: Since I work from home, my schedule’s flexible. I try to finish around 6 in the evening. But sometimes, I’m in the middle of solving a problem, I’ll work later

MAN: The challenge with working from home is separating work life from personal life, isn’t it?

WOMAN: Absolutely, some days I find myself working until 8 or 9 in the evening without realizing it

MAN: You need to set boundaries. Maybe create a specific workspace and leave it when work hours are over

WOMAN: That’s good advice. I have a home office, but I often bring my laptop to the living room

MAN: That makes it harder to disconnect. Try to keep work confined to your office space

WOMAN: You’re right. I try to be more disciplined about that

MAN: It’s important for mental health. Everyone needs time to relax and recharge

WOMAN: Speaking of relaxation. Do you practice any meditation or yoga

/ˈjoʊ.ɡə/

MAN: I tried yoga for a few months last year. It was good, but I couldn’t maintain the routine

WOMAN: Consistency is always the hardest part. I’ve tried meditation several times, but I always stop after a few weeks

MAN: Maybe we could try together; having an accountability partner might help

WOMAN: That’s an interesting idea. There’s probably a yoga studio or meditation center nearby

MAN: Actually, there’s a yoga studio two blocks from here. I’ve walked past it many times

WOMAN: Should we investigate it? Maybe they offer beginner classes

MAN: Let’s do it. We could go after we finish our coffee

WOMAN: Perfect, I’m excited about it. It would be good to add some physical activities to my routine

MAN: Same here, I spent too much time sitting at my desk

WOMAN: Modern life is very sedentary  /ˈsed.ən.ter.i/, isn’t it? Our ancestors were much more active

MAN: Definitely, they walked everywhere and did physical labor. We sit in chairs staring at screens

WOMAN: And then we pay the money to go to gyms to exercise. It’s quite ironic /aɪˈrɑː.nɪk/ when you think about it

MAN: That’s a good point. We’ve created problems that require artificial solutions
